1. Core pitch

30-second version

At Homa, I learned rigorous paid acquisition at scale: large budgets, attribution, ROAS, LTV and creative testing.

At Rivrs, I moved further upstream. I now build the acquisition and creative systems themselves: creators, production, international launches, testing velocity and team processes.

Mirelo is interesting to me because it combines both.

Homa = paid rigor → Rivrs = creative systems → Mirelo = both

2. “What would you test first?”

Say this first

I’d test who we target, what message we use, and how we demonstrate the product. Then I’d look at which combination brings the best activated users — not just the cheapest clicks.

Audience

Who has the strongest pain and existing workflow?

Message

Speed? Quality? Control? Better storytelling?

Creative

Before / after, workflow demo, creator-led, use-case specific.

Quality

Judge on activation and downstream behaviour, not only CTR or signup CAC.

If she asks for segments

My first hypotheses would be video creators, professional editors / filmmakers, and potentially gaming creators. But I’d look at existing user data before deciding where to put most of the budget.

3. “How do you structure a test?”

Keep it simple

One clear question, one main variable, one success metric. Then I document what happened, what we learned, and what we should test next.

If she pushes deeper

For a bigger decision, I also want enough volume and consistency before scaling. And I try to distinguish a real learning from noise.

Hypothesis → Test → Measure → Learn → Next test

4. Growth metrics to master

CACMeaningless alone. Connect it to activation, retention, revenue and payback.
ActivationThe early behaviour that best predicts future retention or value.
RetentionDo acquired users keep coming back and experiencing value?
LTVExpected value generated by a user over time.
PaybackHow long it takes to recover acquisition spend.
Marginal CACWhat the next unit of spend costs — critical when scaling.
IncrementalityDid paid create the outcome, or capture something that would have happened anyway?
AttributionUseful, imperfect. Combine platform signals with first-party product data.
“What is a good CAC?”

There is no good CAC without understanding LTV and payback. I’d start from the economics we want and work backwards.

5. Creative

“How do you evaluate a creative?”

First: does it stop attention? Then: does it create intent? And finally: does that traffic convert and activate? A strong CTR with poor activation is not a winner.

Hook

Does it earn the next few seconds?

Intent

CTR / landing behaviour / product interest.

Conversion

Signup or purchase efficiency.

User quality

Activation, repeat usage, retention.

Key line

I know paid acquisition, but I also know how to build the creative supply behind it.

6. “How would you scale?”

Main answer

I’d scale after we find a repeatable audience, message and creative combination that produces good users. Then I’d increase spend progressively while watching marginal CAC and user quality.

Scaling budget and scaling creative production have to happen together.
Channel expansion

I prefer learning deeply on a small number of channels first, then expanding once we understand what is working and why.

7. Measurement

Ad → Signup → Activation → Repeat use → Paid conversion → Retention
“What is activation for Mirelo?”

I have a hypothesis, but I wouldn’t define it before looking at the data. I’d identify which early behaviour best predicts retention or conversion.

Tracking credibility

I’m comfortable working on the tracking setup, even if I’m not a data engineer. I’ve worked with GTM, GA4, Meta / TikTok pixels, PostHog, Adjust, Singular and Tableau.

Builder mindset

Best Rivrs story

Creative production was becoming the bottleneck. We built repeatable formats, creator workflows and automation. Today we produce 50+ videos per week across multiple accounts and languages. The point is not volume — it is learning faster.

9. Pushback / gaps

“You come from gaming, not SaaS.”

That’s the biggest difference in my background. But the paid fundamentals are very transferable: acquisition economics, attribution, creative testing, segmentation and retention. The part I’d need to learn quickly is Mirelo’s specific user behaviour and subscription economics.

“Do you know SQL?”

I’m functional, but I wouldn’t position myself as an expert. I’m comfortable working with product and analytics data and getting to the answer I need.

“Are you still hands-on?”

Yes. I manage a team, but I still work directly on campaign strategy, creative concepts, analytics and testing. I like defining the system and still being able to go into the account or the data myself.

Do not overclaim

Do not pretend gaming retention = creative SaaS retention. Do not call yourself an SQL expert. Do not say you personally acquired all 1.5M Rivrs users.

10. What Ketty may challenge

Activation

What behaviour actually predicts long-term value?

Retention

Are you acquiring curiosity or people who build a workflow?

Economics

CAC, LTV, payback, marginal CAC.

Incrementality

How do you know paid caused the result?

Creative fatigue

What happens when spend grows faster than fresh concepts?

PMF vs paid

Paid can amplify product value; it cannot manufacture retention.

Strong Mirelo insight

One risk is acquiring curiosity instead of real users. AI demos can drive cheap clicks and signups, but a one-time wow moment is different from becoming part of someone’s workflow.

Strong Mirelo opportunity

Mirelo is highly demonstrable. A silent video versus the generated result can communicate value in seconds, so the creative itself can become a strong distribution mechanism.

11. Questions to ask Ketty

From what you’ve seen so far, where is the biggest growth uncertainty at Mirelo today: audience, positioning, activation or distribution?
What would you want the person in this role to prove in the first three months?
Where do you think paid should sit in Mirelo’s growth engine — mainly as a scaling channel, a learning channel, or both?
What have you seen in the current data that surprised you most?
