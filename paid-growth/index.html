1. Core pitch

This is the story she should remember.

45-second answer

At Homa, I learned how to run paid acquisition at scale. I was managing large budgets across several mobile games, working with Meta, TikTok, Google and mobile ad networks, while tracking ROAS, LTV and attribution very closely.

It also gave me a strong testing mindset: how to structure experiments, compare creative concepts, read performance data and decide what to scale or stop.

At Rivrs, my role became broader and much more creative. I’m now much closer to the full growth system: creative strategy, creator and influencer programs, UGC and content production, international launches, testing speed and team processes.

I manage a team of 7 across 10 games, so a big part of my job is building systems that help us produce and test more ideas, faster, without losing quality.

That is why Mirelo is very interesting to me. It sits exactly at the intersection of my main areas of expertise: creative strategy, influencers and creators, UGC production, and paid acquisition.

I also use AI tools a lot to move faster — for ideation, scripting, localization, automation and creative production. For me, AI is not the strategy itself. It is a way to increase execution speed and testing velocity.

Homa = paid rigor → Rivrs = creative speed → Mirelo = both

2. Ketty's growth lens

These are the ideas you should be ready to discuss. Do not force them into every answer.

Hype is not product-market fit

A lot of attention, clicks or signups does not mean users will stay.

For Mirelo, I would care about repeat use and retention, not only cheap acquisition.

Distribution is bigger than paid ads

Paid is one channel. Distribution can also come from creators, organic content, sharing, communities and product loops.

I would use paid to learn fast and scale what works — not as the only growth engine.

Do not depend on one “winner” forever

A channel, audience or creative can stop working.

The real advantage is a system that keeps finding new messages, new creatives and new ways to distribute the product.

Attention ≠ retention · Paid = learn + scale · Distribution should compound

3. “What would you test first?”

Start broad. Do not pretend you know the answer before seeing Mirelo's data.

Main answer

I’d start with three things: who we target, what message we use, and how we show the product.

Then I’d look at which combination brings the best activated users — not just the cheapest clicks.

Audience

Who has a real need and already creates video often?

Message

What matters most: speed, quality, control, or better storytelling?

Creative

Before / after. Product demo. Creator-led. One clear use case.

User quality

Do they activate, come back and pay?

If she asks for audience ideas

My first hypotheses would be video creators, professional editors or filmmakers, and maybe gaming creators.

But I would first look at the current user data before choosing where to spend most of the budget.

4. “How do you structure a test?”

Simple answer

One clear question. One main variable. One success metric.

Then I write down what we learned and what we should test next.

Question → Test → Result → Learning → Next test
If she pushes

For an important decision, I also want enough volume and a result that is consistent before I scale it.

5. Metrics to master

You only need simple definitions and the link between them.

CACHow much it costs to acquire a customer. It means little without LTV and payback.
ActivationThe first action that shows the user has reached real product value.
RetentionDo users come back and keep using the product?
LTVHow much value or revenue a customer creates over time.
PaybackHow long it takes to earn back the acquisition cost.
Marginal CACWhat the next part of your budget costs when you scale.
IncrementalityDid paid really create the conversion, or would it have happened anyway?
AttributionUseful, but never perfect. Use platform data and product data together.
“What is a good CAC?”

There is no good CAC on its own.

I need to understand LTV and payback first, then work backwards.

6. Creative

This is one of your strongest areas. Keep it connected to business outcomes.

“How do you judge a creative?”

First: does it get attention?

Second: does it create intent?

Third: do those users actually activate and come back?

A high CTR with bad activation is not a winner.

Hook

Does it make people stop?

Intent

CTR, landing behaviour, interest.

Conversion

Signup or purchase efficiency.

Quality

Activation, repeat use, retention.

Your strongest line

I know paid acquisition, but I also know how to build the creative supply behind it.

7. Distribution & scaling

This is where you can connect your Rivrs experience to Ketty's way of thinking.

“How would you scale?”

I would first find a repeatable combination of audience, message and creative that brings good users.

Then I would increase spend step by step, while watching marginal CAC and user quality.

Scaling budget and scaling creative have to happen together.
Paid + distribution

I see paid as both a learning channel and a scaling channel.

But I would also look for distribution we can build over time: creators, organic content and product sharing.

Strong Mirelo thought

Mirelo is easy to demonstrate. You can show the value in a few seconds with a before / after video.

That means the creative can also become part of the distribution.

8. Future growth levers I like

If she asks where you see the next strong opportunities, keep it simple and connected to learning quality.

OpenAI / AI discovery

I’m very interested in both paid and organic opportunities around AI discovery. It is becoming a new place where users look for tools and recommendations.

Organic growth

I like channels where content keeps working over time and where we can learn without paying for every impression.

Creators

Creators often give the best signals. They show us which hooks, use cases and messages feel natural before we scale them with paid.

Paid acquisition

Paid is still very useful for fast learning and scale — especially once we know which message and audience already show strong signals.

Simple way to say it

For the future, I’m very interested in the mix of AI discovery, organic growth, creators and paid.

Creators and organic often give the best early signals. Then paid can help us validate faster and scale what is already working.

11. Pushback

“You come from gaming, not SaaS.”

That is the biggest difference in my background.

But the core skills transfer well: acquisition economics, creative testing, attribution, segmentation and retention.

What I need to learn fast is Mirelo’s specific user behaviour and subscription model.

“Do you know SQL?”

I can work with data and get to the answer I need, but I would not call myself an SQL expert.

“Are you still hands-on?”

Yes. I manage a team, but I still work directly on campaign strategy, creative ideas, analytics and testing.

“What is the risk with paid for an AI product?”

Acquiring curiosity instead of real users.

A great AI demo can create a lot of clicks, but that is not the same as building a habit or a workflow.
